# RFC-077: The Capability Split

Status: **Accepted 2026-11-03, by inheritance — split out of RFC-074 on 2026-11-12 at the owner's
direction.** D5 through D8 below are the decisions the owner accepted as part of RFC-074 ("D1
through D8 as written"); they are carried here **unchanged and still accepted**. The split was not
a re-opening: it exists because RFC-074 spanned more releases than one, and a released section
naming an RFC asserts that RFC shipped in that release, so the first slice could not close while
the capability split was still unstarted — the same reason RFC-026 was split from RFC-065.

**Not scheduled, deliberately.** This RFC follows [RFC-074](../done/074-capability-boundaries.md)'s first
slice (`0.40.0`: the gate, `ProjectId` to T0, `test_support` out of the production graph) and its
staging is scoped **after** that gate has behaved on two real stages — exactly the condition
RFC-074's own *Acceptance* set, unchanged by the split. Scoping twelve stages before the first one
lands is the kind of plan this project's history says gets rewritten.

**What the owner still decides**: the release slot, when the staging is scoped. The `project/`
split may not be API-compatible, which is a separate question from the one answered for RFC-074's
first slice.

## Summary

`project/` is not one capability: it is an orchestrator plus six or seven separately-authored ones
sharing a directory. This RFC holds the three decisions that govern how they come apart — what a
capability *is*, what it may reach, and in what order the cuts happen — and derives its staging from
measured cut cost rather than preference.

It depends on RFC-074 having landed, because every decision here is enforced by RFC-074's gate and
expressed as a lowering of its bound. Without that gate each move is undone by the next person who
writes a `use`.

## Decisions

**D5, capability isolation, is not here — it is [RFC-074](../done/074-capability-boundaries.md)'s.**
It was moved there on 2026-11-12, in the same pass as this split, because its **first real
application is RFC-074's own Stage 2**: the two `test_support` references are disposed of by D5's
three legal moves, and how that rule is exercised on two trivial references is the precedent for
how it is exercised on forty. A release should be able to cite the rule it applied without naming
an RFC that has not shipped. Every decision below depends on it and none of them weakens it.

### D6. The capability list is generated by a rule, not chosen by taste. **Decided (goal document D-G2).**

> **One capability = one external contract + one grant decision + one threat surface.**

Where any of the three differ, the capabilities are separate; where all three coincide, merging is
correct. Applied, this keeps filesystem safety, file watching and the explorer as **three**
(different external dependencies — none / `notify` / the `git` subprocess; different threat
surfaces; and decisively different Appendix A grant classes — `Read`, `Process`, and host safety
that is never granted at all). Merging them would mint exactly the broad capability Appendix A
§6.1 forbids.

### D7. `project/` splits: orchestration plus its capabilities. **Decided (goal document D-G1).**

Not because the hub is untidy, but because a hub is where safety invariants stop being locatable.
The cost of this split only ever rises — `project/` is ~9,041 production lines today, and every
capability added after 1.0 attaches to the hub.

### D8. Staging order is derived from measured cut cost, not from preference

After Stage 1, the cheapest single cuts that each take the component from 9 to 8, measured:

| Cut | References to remove |
| --- | --- |
| `runtime → test_support` | 1 |
| `security → project` | 1 |
| `test_support → runtime` | 1 |
| `content → project` | 4 |
| `approval → audit` | 5 |

Each stage: one cut or one coherent group, the gate's bound lowered in the same commit, the full
suite and both platforms green, and the six `ARCHITECTURE.md` invariants intact. **A stage that
cannot lower the bound is not a stage** — it is a move that should be folded into one that can.

The later stages (the `project/` split proper) are not enumerated here. They will be, in a
handoff pack, once Stages 1–2 have shown the gate behaves and the cut costs above survive contact.
Scoping twelve stages before the first one lands would be the kind of plan this project's own
history says gets rewritten.

## Out of scope, deliberately

Inherited from RFC-074 and unchanged: this is **not a crate split** (crates are a packaging
decision, available once the properties hold), **not the GUI's breadth** (`crates/tekstide`
references 21 distinct `tekstide_core` modules — a different question, needing its own RFC and its
own reason), **not a rename**, and **not plugins** (the MVP ships no plugin runtime; this work only
requires that the seams *could* be added without rewriting).

## Acceptance

**Accepted already**, on 2026-11-03, as D5–D8 of RFC-074. What remains is not acceptance but
scoping: the later stages are enumerated in a handoff pack once RFC-074's Stages 1 and 2 have shown
the gate behaves and the cut costs in D8 survive contact. **A stage that cannot lower the gate's
bound is not a stage** — it is a move that should be folded into one that can.
